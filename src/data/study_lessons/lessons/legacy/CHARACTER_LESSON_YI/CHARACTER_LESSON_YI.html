以 (yǐ) — Common Words & Grammatical Uses

Category: Vocabulary & Grammar Patterns
Topics: lexicalization, formal register, prepositions

Everyday compounds, formal patterns, and register

Learn established words containing 以 (yǐ), including 可以 (kěyǐ), 所以 (suǒyǐ), and 以为 (yǐwéi), then explore formal grammatical constructions, lexicalization, and register differences from 用 (yòng).

Lesson focus: Learn common complete words containing 以 (yǐ), then recognize formal patterns expressing means or taking one thing as another.

Core principle: A character is a writing unit, a morpheme is a meaningful unit, a word is a lexical item, and a construction is a reusable grammatical pattern. Do not conflate them.

Learning path: Study natural modern words first; formal constructions come second, and Classical Chinese is optional.

1. At a glance

Feature Description
Character 以 (yǐ)
Core takeaway Occurs frequently in established words such as 可以 (kěyǐ), 所以 (suǒyǐ), 以前 (yǐqián), and 以后 (yǐhòu).
Independent usage Most accessible in established words; independent grammatical use is especially formal or written.
Modern functions Element of compound words; Preposition in formal Mandarin; Part of productive constructions
Traditional form(s) 以 (yǐ)

Main uses

Reading Function Register Example
yǐ Element in established lexicalized words Spoken and written 可以 (kěyǐ) — may; can
yǐ By; by means of Relatively formal/written 以书面形式 (yǐ shūmiàn xíngshì) — in written form
yǐ Take X as Y Formal/written; some common subpatterns 以他为榜样 (yǐ tā wéi bǎngyàng) — take him as a role model
yǐ Historical grammatical functions Classical Chinese only —

The character does not map to one English word in every context. It participates in many complete words and some reusable constructions; learn the complete modern words before trying to interpret the character in isolation.

2. Essential vocabulary and expressions

Priority Word and pinyin Part of speech Meaning Register Usage
A 可以 (kěyǐ) modal verb Can; may; be permitted to everyday Lexicalized modal verb; the historical components are not a productive modern rule.
A 所以 (suǒyǐ) conjunction So; therefore everyday An established connective expressing a result or conclusion.
A 以后 (yǐhòu) temporal expression After; later; in the future everyday Often follows a time phrase or an event.
A 以前 (yǐqián) temporal expression Before; previously; in the past everyday Often used before or after a temporal reference.
A 以为 (yǐwéi) verb Think; assume everyday Often implies that an assumption was wrong, but this is a tendency, not a rule.
B 以上 (yǐshàng) localizer / range expression Above; at least; or more neutral Boundary inclusion can depend on the construction and context.
B 以下 (yǐxià) localizer / range expression Below; at most; or less neutral Boundary interpretation depends on context.
B 以内 (yǐnèi) range expression Within; inside a limit neutral Time, space, or quantity.
B 以外 (yǐwài) range expression Outside; apart from; except neutral Location or exclusion depends on context.
C 以及 (yǐjí) conjunction As well as; and written/formal Connects parallel terms or groups.
C 以便 (yǐbiàn) conjunction So that; in order to written/formal Expresses a desirable purpose or convenience.
C 以免 (yǐmiǎn) conjunction So as to avoid; lest written/formal Introduces an undesirable outcome to prevent.

以为 (yǐwéi) is one lexical verb, not a shortened instance of 以 X 为 Y (yǐ X wéi Y). 可以 (kěyǐ) is likewise an established lexicalized modal; neither provides a freely productive pattern based on historical component meanings.

3. Grammar patterns and constructions

Pattern Function Example Register Usage note
以 + 名词短语 (yǐ + míngcí duǎnyǔ) Using or by a particular means 以书面形式提交 (yǐ shūmiàn xíngshì tíjiāo) — Submit in writing formal/written Abstract means and manner are frequent; it is not restricted to abstract nouns.
以 X 为 Y (yǐ X wéi Y) Take or treat X as Y 以他为榜样 (yǐ tā wéi bǎngyàng) — Take him as a role model mainly written/formal X and Y denote variable constituents, not necessarily single characters.
以 X 为主 (yǐ X wéi zhǔ) Make X the principal focus 以学习为主 (yǐ xuéxí wéi zhǔ) — Focus mainly on studying neutral-to-formal Keep the fixed element 为主 (wéi zhǔ) distinct from the general slots.
以 X 为例 (yǐ X wéi lì) Use X as an example 以中国为例 (yǐ Zhōngguó wéi lì) — Take China as an example expository/formal Keep the fixed element 为例 (wéi lì) distinct from the general slots.

The reusable pattern 以 X 为 Y (yǐ X wéi Y) permits variable constituents; 以 X 为主 (yǐ X wéi zhǔ) and 以 X 为例 (yǐ X wéi lì) are recurring subpatterns. The standalone lexicalized verb 以为 (yǐwéi) is a different grammatical unit.

4. Natural example sentences

1. Asking permission

我可以进去吗？

Wǒ kěyǐ jìnqù ma?

May I come in?

Grammar: Treat the two-character modal as a single word.

Context: Asking to enter a room.

2. Explaining a result

今天下雨，所以我不出去。

Jīntiān xià yǔ, suǒyǐ wǒ bù chūqù.

It's raining today, so I'm not going out.

Grammar: A connective linking reason and result.

Context: Explaining a decision.

3. After a time

我三点以后有时间。

Wǒ sān diǎn yǐhòu yǒu shíjiān.

I'm free after three o'clock.

Grammar: The temporal word follows a time expression.

Context: Arranging a meeting.

4. Talking about the past

我以前住在北京。

Wǒ yǐqián zhù zài Běijīng.

I used to live in Beijing.

Grammar: Marks an earlier period; the present location remains unspecified.

Context: Discussing former residence.

5. A potentially wrong assumption

我以为你不来了。

Wǒ yǐwéi nǐ bù lái le.

I thought you weren't coming.

Grammar: The assumption commonly turns out wrong in such contexts, but this is not mandatory.

Context: Someone unexpectedly shows up.

6. Formal instructions

请以书面形式提交申请。

Qǐng yǐ shūmiàn xíngshì tíjiāo shēnqǐng.

Please submit your application in writing.

Grammar: A means or manner prepositional phrase.

Context: Institutional instructions.

7. Main focus of a course

这门课以口语练习为主。

Zhè mén kè yǐ kǒuyǔ liànxí wéi zhǔ.

This course focuses mainly on speaking practice.

Grammar: Recognize the established subpattern as a unit.

Context: Course description.

5. Comparisons and distinctions

Expression Meaning Example Usage Important distinction
用 (yòng) Use; with 用筷子吃饭 (yòng kuàizi chīfàn) — Eat with chopsticks Natural with concrete instruments in ordinary speech. Frequently conversational; not freely interchangeable with formal means-constructions.
以 (yǐ) By; by means of 以这种方式 (yǐ zhè zhǒng fāngshì) — In this way Often describes abstract means or manners. Especially written/formal in instrumental use; concrete nouns are not categorically excluded.
以为 (yǐwéi) Think; assume 我以为你不来了 (Wǒ yǐwéi nǐ bù lái le) — I thought you weren't coming. Often used when an expectation is contradicted. That implication is common but not obligatory.
认为 (rènwéi) Consider; hold the opinion 我认为这个办法不错 (Wǒ rènwéi zhège bànfǎ búcuò) — I think this approach is good. Often for an assessment or opinion. Does not inherently carry the same mistaken-expectation tendency.
觉得 (juéde) Feel; think 我觉得很好 (Wǒ juéde hěn hǎo) — I think it's good. Common for subjective impressions. Different usage emphasis from holding an argued opinion.

In the instrumental-preposition subset of Sun Dejin’s corpus, abstract nouns followed 以 (yǐ) in 166 of 189 occurrences (about 88%) and 用 (yòng) in 146 of 319 (about 46%). Concrete-noun observations were 23 and 173 respectively. These are corpus-specific collocational preferences, not universal restrictions or contemporary language-wide frequency estimates.

6. Common learner mistakes

Mistake Correct approach Explanation
Translate every occurrence of 以 (yǐ) as “using”. Identify the complete word or grammatical construction first. For instance, 所以 (suǒyǐ) is an established conjunction meaning “so; therefore”.
Apply 可 (kě) + 以 (yǐ) as a productive rule to form new modal verbs. Learn 可以 (kěyǐ) as one word. Historical lexicalization does not create a general productive beginner rule.
Treat 以为 (yǐwéi) as an unfilled 以 X 为 Y (yǐ X wéi Y) pattern. Differentiate lexicalized words and grammatical constructions. The modern verb is a lexical unit, whereas the pattern is a construction.
Assume 以为 (yǐwéi) always means a mistaken assumption. Interpret the discourse context. Often—but not invariably—suggests a contradicted assumption.
Swap 以 (yǐ) and 用 (yòng) in every sentence. Check register and noun collocations. Comparable English glosses do not imply unrestricted interchangeability.

7. Optional: Classical Chinese

Optional Classical Chinese reference. Pinyin shown is a modern Mandarin reading, not reconstructed ancient pronunciation.

Function Example Meaning Explanation
Marks the thing transferred or handled; approximate overlap with some modern 把 (bǎ) constructions. 秦亦不以城予赵。 (Qín yì bù yǐ chéng yǔ Zhào.) Qin likewise did not give the cities to Zhao. The phrase 以城 (yǐ chéng) identifies what would be given. The comparison with 把 (bǎ) is restricted to this kind of construction.

Note: Do not treat classical 以 (yǐ) and modern 把 (bǎ) as generally synonymous or freely interchangeable.

8. Practice

Question 1

In 我以前住在北京 (Wǒ yǐqián zhù zài Běijīng), does the speaker refer to present or earlier residence?

Question 2

Which sounds natural for casual “eat with chopsticks”?

  • A. 用筷子吃饭 (yòng kuàizi chīfàn) — Eat with chopsticks
  • B. 以筷子吃饭 (yǐ kuàizi chīfàn) — Unnatural as an everyday conversational phrasing

Question 3

Translate “May I come in?” using 可以 (kěyǐ).

Suggested answers
  1. Earlier residence; current location is unspecified. — The temporal expression 以前 (yǐqián) points to an earlier period.
  2. A. 用筷子吃饭 (yòng kuàizi chīfàn) — Eat with chopsticks — 用 (yòng) is the ordinary choice with a concrete tool in this casual context.
  3. 我可以进去吗？(Wǒ kěyǐ jìnqù ma?) — The complete modal verb expresses permission.
