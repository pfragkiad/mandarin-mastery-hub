Conversational Mandarin 15 min

Asking About Prices

Prices in yuan and asking how much something costs

Learn to ask the prices of common drinks and reply with amounts in renminbi.

Learning objectives

  • Ask how much something costs.
  • Say amounts in yuan or US dollars.
  • Use the additional situational conversation to apply the lesson in a longer exchange.

Study guidance

Learn to ask the prices of common drinks and reply with amounts in renminbi. Use the model conversation to notice the expressions, then practice the patterns and speak aloud.

Model conversation

A

请问，一杯啤酒多少钱？

Qǐngwèn, yì bēi píjiǔ duōshao qián?

Excuse me, how much is a glass of beer?

B

十块人民币。

Shí kuài rénmínbì.

Ten yuan.

A

你有多少钱？

Nǐ yǒu duōshao qián?

How much money do you have?

B

我有二十块人民币。

Wǒ yǒu èrshí kuài rénmínbì.

I have twenty yuan.

Grammar structure and spoken example

物品 + 多少钱？ (wùpǐn + duōshao qián?)

多少钱 (duōshao qián) asks for the price.

一杯茶多少钱？ (yì bēi chá duōshao qián?) — How much is a cup of tea?

Name the item when checking a quoted price.

Grammar structure and spoken example

数量 + 块 + 人民币 (shùliàng + kuài + rénmínbì)

块 (kuài) is the colloquial unit used to count yuan or a currency amount.

我有十块人民币。 (wǒ yǒu shí kuài rénmínbì.) — I have ten yuan.

In ordinary conversations, 块 (kuài) is the casual word for yuan.

Study guidance

Speaking task: Ask the price of tea or beer; answer with a simple amount in RMB or dollars.

Comparing Currency and Cash on Hand

A

这件东西十八块人民币。

Zhè jiàn dōngxi shíbā kuài rénmínbì.

This item costs eighteen yuan.

B

太多钱了！我只有八块，不够。

Tài duō qián le! Wǒ zhǐ yǒu bā kuài, bú gòu.

That is too much money! I only have eight yuan, not enough.

A

你有美金吗？

Nǐ yǒu měijīn ma?

Do you have US dollars?

B

没有。可是我朋友有很多人民币。

Méiyǒu. Kěshì wǒ péngyou yǒu hěn duō rénmínbì.

No. But my friend has plenty of yuan.

Grammar structure and spoken example

只有 + 数量 + 钱；不够 (zhǐ yǒu + shùliàng + qián; bú gòu)

只有 (zhǐ yǒu) emphasizes a limited amount; 够 (gòu) means enough.

我只有五块钱，不够。 (Wǒ zhǐ yǒu wǔ kuài qián, bú gòu.) — I only have five yuan; that is not enough.

This gives a practical reason for being unable to pay the requested amount.
