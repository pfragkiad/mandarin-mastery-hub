1 · Conversation — Food and Drink

Two friends order lunch and discuss what they do and do not want to eat.

A

你想吃什么？ (Nǐ xiǎng chī shénme?)

What would you like to eat?

B

我想吃饺子，喝一杯茶。 (Wǒ xiǎng chī jiǎozi, hē yì bēi chá.)

I would like dumplings and a cup of tea.

A

你还要米饭吗？ (Nǐ hái yào mǐfàn ma?)

Would you like rice as well?

B

不要了，谢谢！ (Bú yào le, xièxie!)

No more, thank you!

2 · How the language works

Food and drink verbs

Use 吃 (chī) for eating and 喝 (hē) for drinking. 想 (xiǎng) makes a polite expression of what you would like.

我想 + 吃/喝 + 食物/饮料
wǒ xiǎng + chī/hē + shíwù/yǐnliào
我想 + 吃/喝 + <food>/<drink>

我晚上想吃面条儿。 (Wǒ wǎnshang xiǎng chī miàntiáor.)

I would like noodles this evening.

她想喝牛奶。 (Tā xiǎng hē niúnǎi.)

She would like milk.

Offering and declining

要 (yào) can indicate wanting an item. Adding 还 (hái) asks whether someone wants more; 不要了 (bú yào le) politely closes an offer.

还要 + 物品 + 吗？
hái yào + wùpǐn + ma?
还要 + <item> + 吗？

你还要水吗？ (Nǐ hái yào shuǐ ma?)

Would you like more water?

我不要了，谢谢。 (Wǒ bú yào le, xièxie.)

I do not want any more, thank you.

3 · Build new sentences

Keep the request frame, but change what is being ordered.

Worked model

我想喝一杯茶。 (Wǒ xiǎng hē yì bēi chá.)

I would like a cup of tea.

Change 1: water
Reveal a model response

我想喝一杯水。 (Wǒ xiǎng hē yì bēi shuǐ.)

I would like a glass of water.

Change 2: milk
Reveal a model response

我想喝一杯牛奶。 (Wǒ xiǎng hē yì bēi niúnǎi.)

I would like a glass of milk.

4 · Read for meaning — A short connected story

Read the passage, then answer in English or Mandarin.

早上我吃鸡蛋和面包，中午吃米饭和菜。晚上爸爸做饭，妈妈和我一起吃。 (Zǎoshang wǒ chī jīdàn hé miànbāo, zhōngwǔ chī mǐfàn hé cài. Wǎnshang bàba zuò fàn, māma hé wǒ yìqǐ chī.)

I eat eggs and bread in the morning, rice and dishes at noon. In the evening Dad cooks, and Mom and I eat together.

1. What does the speaker eat for breakfast?
Show the answer

Eggs and bread.

2. Who cooks dinner?
Show the answer

Dad.

6 · Watch out

吃 (chī) refers to eating and 喝 (hē) to drinking. “喝米饭” is not an ordinary way to describe eating rice.

Pronunciation clinic

A measure phrase such as 一杯 (yì bēi) uses yì before a first-tone word.
