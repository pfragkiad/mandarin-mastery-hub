Question Words, This and That

1 · Conversation — Asking and Pointing

At a bookstore, one friend asks which object and where; the other uses near/far demonstratives.

A

你要哪本书？ (Nǐ yào nǎ běn shū?)

Which book do you want?

B

我要这本。 (Wǒ yào zhè běn.)

I want this one.

A

那本呢？ (Nà běn ne?)

What about that one?

B

那本太贵了。 (Nà běn tài guì le.)

That one is too expensive.

2 · How the language works

This versus that

这 (zhè) points to something closer in the discourse or situation; 那 (nà) points to something more distant. Add a classifier when identifying a specific item.

这/那 + classifier + noun

这个苹果很好吃。 (Zhège píngguǒ hěn hǎochī.)

This apple is tasty.

那件衣服很漂亮。 (Nà jiàn yīfu hěn piàoliang.)

That item of clothing is pretty.

Choose the right question word

什么 (shénme) asks “what”; 谁 (shéi) asks “who”; 哪 (nǎ) asks “which”; 哪里 (nǎlǐ) asks “where”; 怎么 (zěnme) asks “how”.

谁 + verb? / 在 + 哪里?

谁是你的老师？ (Shéi shì nǐ de lǎoshī?)

Who is your teacher?

你住在哪里？ (Nǐ zhù zài nǎlǐ?)

Where do you live?

Move from a general what-question to a which-question.

Worked model

What do you want to buy?

Change 1: which book
Reveal a model response

6 · Watch out

哪 (nǎ, which) and 那 (nà, that) are different characters and tones. Do not write 那本 (nà běn) when asking “which book?”.

Pronunciation clinic

In spontaneous speech, 谁 has common readings shéi and shuí; avoid treating them as different words.
