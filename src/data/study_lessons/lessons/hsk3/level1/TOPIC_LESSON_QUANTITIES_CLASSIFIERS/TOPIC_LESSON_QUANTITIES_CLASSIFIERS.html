Counting Real Things

Classifiers, Amounts and Approximate Quantities

numbers-quantities · shopping-money · #classifiers · #amounts · #approximation

Learning objectives

  • Use common classifiers with familiar nouns.
  • Differentiate exact and approximate quantities.
  • Understand expressions of halves, a little and some.

Overview

Count familiar objects and describe quantities with classifiers and expressions of amount.

Use common classifiers with familiar nouns.

Vocabulary and examples

HSK entry 7
半 (bàn)

half

numeral · introduced

现在是八点半。 (xiànzài shì bā diǎn bàn.)

It is half past eight now.

八点半 is the ordinary way to say 8:30; 半 means half an hour.

学校上午八点半上课。 (xuéxiào shàngwǔ bā diǎn bàn shàngkè.)

Classes at the school start at 8:30 a.m.

Primarily the numeral “half” (半个小时, 八点半). Also has a restricted adverbial role meaning partly/half- in advanced patterns; that secondary role is not a Level 1 teaching priority.

HSK entry 10
本 (běn)

measure word for books

classifier · introduced

我买了两本书。 (wǒ mǎi le liǎng běn shū.)

I bought two books.

本 is the standard classifier for books; 两 normally precedes classifiers rather than 二.

学生每人有一本书。 (xuéshēng měi rén yǒu yì běn shū.)

Every student has one book.

HSK entry 31
第 (dì)

ordinal prefix

prefix · introduced

这是第一课吗？ (zhè shì dì yī kè ma?)

Is this the first lesson?

今天我们上第一课。 (jīntiān wǒmen shàng dì yī kè.)

Today we are having our first lesson.

Ordinal prefix before numerals.

HSK entry 60
个 (gè)

general measure word

classifier · introduced

我有一个朋友。 (wǒ yǒu yí ge péngyou.)

I have a friend.

个 is the common classifier for 朋友; 一 is normally pronounced yí before this neutral-tone 个.

这个班有二十个学生。 (zhège bān yǒu èr shí gè xuéshēng.)

There are twenty students in this class.

HSK entry 85
几 (jǐ)

how many

pronoun · introduced

你有几个朋友？ (nǐ yǒu jǐ gè péngyou?)

How many friends do you have?

几 asks “how many” for a relatively small count and is followed by a classifier.

今天来了几个学生？ (jīntiān lái le jǐ gè xuéshēng?)

How many students came today?

Interrogative quantity pronoun/numeral meaning how many (几个人、几本书). 个 or 本 is the classifier; 几 is NOT a classifier.

HSK entry 89
件 (jiàn)

measure word for items

classifier · introduced

我买了一件衣服。 (wǒ mǎi le yí jiàn yīfu.)

I bought an item of clothing.

件 is the measure word for individual items of clothing.

我有三件新衣服。 (wǒ yǒu sān jiàn xīn yīfu.)

I have three new items of clothing.

HSK entry 111
两 (liǎng)

two (quantity)

numeral · introduced

我有两个孩子。 (wǒ yǒu liǎng gè háizi.)

I have two children.

两 is used before 个 and other classifiers to mean “two.”

学校有两个大教室。 (xuéxiào yǒu liǎng gè dà jiàoshì.)

The school has two large classrooms.

HSK entry 239
些 (xiē)

some

classifier · introduced

我想买些水果。 (wǒ xiǎng mǎi xiē shuǐguǒ.)

I would like to buy some fruit.

些 expresses an unspecified plural amount, here some fruit.

这里有些书，也有些电脑。 (zhèlǐ yǒuxiē shū, yě yǒuxiē diànnǎo.)

There are some books here, as well as some computers.

Indefinite quantity word, conventionally classified as a classifier/quantifier.

HSK entry 258
一半 (yíbàn)

half

numeral · introduced

这个苹果我吃了一半。 (zhège píngguǒ wǒ chī le yíbàn.)

I ate half of this apple.

The apple is introduced as the topic; 一半 states that half was eaten.

这本书我读了一半。 (zhè běn shū wǒ dú le yíbàn.)

I've read half of this book.

Fractional numeral phrase meaning one half.

HSK entry 259
一下 (yíxià)

a moment; briefly

quantifier · introduced

请等一下。 (qǐng děng yíxià.)

Please wait a moment.

等一下 is a polite request to wait a moment; 一下 softens the command.

“请看一下这个字。”老师说。 (“qǐng kàn yíxià zhège zì.” lǎoshī shuō.)

"Please take a look at this character," the teacher said.

Written dialogue: 一下 is used naturally in a spoken exchange, rather than forced into formal prose.

Verb-quantity complement meaning briefly/once; also adverbial in some uses.

HSK entry 261
一点儿 (yìdiǎnr)

a little

quantifier · introduced

我会说一点儿汉语。 (wǒ huì shuō yìdiǎnr Hànyǔ.)

I can speak a little Chinese.

一点儿 means a little; the 儿 ending is written and pronounced in this form.

请给我一点儿水。 (qǐng gěi wǒ yìdiǎnr shuǐ.)

Please give me a little water.

Quantity expression a little, typically used in object phrases.

HSK entry 262
一些 (yìxiē)

some

quantifier · introduced

我买了一些苹果。 (wǒ mǎi le yìxiē píngguǒ.)

I bought some apples.

一些 means some or several; it precedes the noun 苹果.

这里有一些中文书。 (zhèlǐ yǒu yìxiē Zhōngwén shū.)

There are some books in Chinese here.

Quantity expression some.

HSK entry 264
有的 (yǒude)

some

pronoun · introduced

有的人喜欢茶。 (yǒude rén xǐhuan chá.)

Some people like tea.

有的 before a noun means some members of a group, here some people.

有的学生喜欢唱歌，有的喜欢读书。 (yǒude xuéshēng xǐhuan chàng gē, yǒude xǐhuan dúshū.)

Some students like singing, while others like reading.

Indefinite/pronominal expression meaning some of a group (有的人、有的学生). The Level 1 grammar outline groups 有的 among demonstrative pronouns; it is not a degree adverb.

HSK entry 265
有点儿 (yǒudiǎnr)

somewhat

adverb · introduced

今天有点儿冷。 (jīntiān yǒudiǎnr lěng.)

It is a little cold today.

有点儿 often introduces a slight, sometimes undesirable, condition such as cold.

这个饭店的菜有点儿贵。 (zhège fàndiàn de cài yǒudiǎnr guì.)

The food at this restaurant is a little expensive.

Degree adverb meaning a little; often conveys an undesirable degree.

HSK entry 266
有些 (yǒuxiē)

some

pronoun · introduced

有些学生不在。 (yǒuxiē xuéshēng bú zài.)

Some students are absent.

有些 means some; here it modifies the group of students.

有些孩子今天没有来上学。 (yǒuxiē háizi jīntiān méiyǒu lái shàngxué.)

Some children didn't come to school today.

At beginner level, pronominal/determiner use means some or certain (有些学生). A secondary degree-adverb use means somewhat (有些冷), included as later-level usage.

HSK entry 287
只 (zhī)

measure word for animals; only

classifier · introduced

这里有两只猫。 (zhèlǐ yǒu liǎng zhī māo.)

There are two cats here.

只 is the classifier for animals; here 两只猫 means two cats.

那只猫喜欢玩。 (nà zhī māo xǐhuan wán.)

That cat likes playing.

Classifier for animals and certain objects; not the adverb 只 (zhǐ) meaning only.

Usage and grammar

Numeral + classifier + noun

A classifier typically links a number to a countable noun.

我有一个朋友。 (wǒ yǒu yí ge péngyou.)

I have a friend.

个 is the common classifier for 朋友; 一 is normally pronounced yí before this neutral-tone 个.

Two before classifiers

The quantity two generally uses the special form before a classifier.

我有两个孩子。 (wǒ yǒu liǎng gè háizi.)

I have two children.

两 is used before 个 and other classifiers to mean “two.”
