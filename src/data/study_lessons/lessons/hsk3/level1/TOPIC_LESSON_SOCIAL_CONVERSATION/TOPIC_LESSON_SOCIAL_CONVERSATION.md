# Greeting and Talking to People

Greet people, ask politely, apologize, thank others and talk about everyday matters.

> A teaching lesson: contextual communication, grammar, guided production, reading and speaking. Word-detail cards hold the original two examples per entry.

## Your goal

- Greet someone and say goodbye.

- Thank, apologize and respond naturally.

- Ask a polite question and refer to a matter or issue.

## 1. Conversation — Greeting and Talking to People

Use realistic first-meeting and telephone exchanges. Focus on conventional replies, not word-for-word translation.

**A:** 喂，你好！请问，李老师在吗？ (Wèi, nǐ hǎo! Qǐngwèn, Lǐ lǎoshī zài ma?) — Hello. May I ask if Teacher Li is there?

**B:** 在。请问，您是谁？ (Zài. Qǐngwèn, nín shì shéi?) — Yes. May I ask who you are?

**A:** 我是王明的朋友。 (Wǒ shì Wáng Míng de péngyou.) — I am Wang Ming’s friend.

**B:** 好的，请等一下。 (Hǎo de, qǐng děng yíxià.) — All right, please wait a moment.

## 2. How the language works

### Polite openings

请问 (qǐngwèn) introduces a polite request or question. 您 (nín) shows respect. At the start of a phone conversation 喂 (wèi) functions as a greeting/attention-getter.

**Pattern**

请问 + 问题  
*qǐngwèn + wèntí*  
**请问** + &lt;question&gt;

**请问，您认识王老师吗？**
*Qǐngwèn, nín rènshi Wáng lǎoshī ma?*
Excuse me, do you know Teacher Wang?

**请问，医院在哪里？**
*Qǐngwèn, yīyuàn zài nǎlǐ?*
Excuse me, where is the hospital?

### Apologies and thanks

Use the conventional response matched to the situation: 对不起 (duìbuqǐ) → 没关系 (méi guānxi); 谢谢 (xièxie) → 不客气 (bú kèqi).

**Pattern**

对不起 → 没关系 / 谢谢 → 不客气  
*duìbuqǐ → méi guānxi / xièxie → bú kèqi*  
**对不起** → **没关系** / **谢谢** → **不客气**

**对不起，我今天来晚了。**
*Duìbuqǐ, wǒ jīntiān lái wǎn le.*
Sorry, I am late today.

**没关系，我们现在开始吧。**
*Méi guānxi, wǒmen xiànzài kāishǐ ba.*
That is all right. Let us start now.

## 3. Build new sentences

Match a social expression to a conventional response.

**Worked model:** **谢谢！→ 不客气！**
*Xièxie! → Bú kèqi!*
Thanks! → You’re welcome!

**Change 1: apology**

*Model response:* **对不起！→ 没关系！**
*Duìbuqǐ! → Méi guānxi!*
Sorry! → No problem!

**Change 2: farewell**

*Model response:* **再见！→ 再见！**
*Zàijiàn! → Zàijiàn!*
Goodbye! → Goodbye!

## 4. Read for meaning — A short connected story

**今天小李第一次见王老师。他说：“您好，很高兴认识您！”王老师说：“我也很高兴。”**
*Jīntiān Xiǎo Lǐ dì yī cì jiàn Wáng lǎoshī. Tā shuō: “Nín hǎo, hěn gāoxìng rènshi nín!” Wáng lǎoshī shuō: “Wǒ yě hěn gāoxìng.”*
Today Xiao Li meets Teacher Wang for the first time. He says, “Hello, pleased to meet you!” Teacher Wang replies, “I am pleased too.”

**Q1. Who is Xiao Li meeting?** Suggested answer: Teacher Wang.

**Q2. Have they met before?** Suggested answer: No, this is their first meeting.

## 5. Speak and personalize

Try to answer aloud before checking the sample; adapt it to your own situation.

**Speaking 1:** Introduce yourself politely on the phone.

*Possible response:* **您好，我是小王。**
*Nín hǎo, wǒ shì Xiǎo Wáng.*
Hello, this is Xiao Wang.

**Speaking 2:** Apologize for being late and give a fitting response.

*Possible response:* **对不起，我来晚了。没关系。**
*Duìbuqǐ, wǒ lái wǎn le. Méi guānxi.*
Sorry, I am late. That is all right.

## 6. Watch out

Do not answer 谢谢 (xièxie) with 没关系 (méi guānxi) as your default. 不客气 (bú kèqi) is the standard “you are welcome” reply.

**Pronunciation:** The tone sequence in 你好 (nǐ hǎo) normally involves third-tone sandhi in connected speech.
