Greeting and Talking to People

Greetings, Courtesy and Everyday Questions

communication-social · abstract-concepts · #greetings · #politeness · #social-expressions

Learning objectives

  • Greet someone and say goodbye.
  • Thank, apologize and respond naturally.
  • Ask a polite question and refer to a matter or issue.

Overview

Greet people, ask politely, apologize, thank others and talk about everyday matters.

Greet someone and say goodbye.

Vocabulary and examples

HSK entry 14
不客气 (bú kèqi)

you're welcome

fixed-expression · introduced

不客气！ (bú kèqi!)

You're welcome!

不客气 is a standard spoken response to thanks; the short answer is a complete utterance.

她说：“不客气。” (tā shuō: “bú kèqi.”)

She said, "You're welcome."

Written dialogue: 不客气 is used naturally in a spoken exchange, rather than forced into formal prose.

Conventional response to thanks; classify as a fixed expression, not by the characters separately.

HSK entry 24
打电话 (dǎ diànhuà)

make a phone call

verb · introduced

我要给妈妈打电话。 (wǒ yào gěi māma dǎ diànhuà.)

I want to call my mother.

给妈妈 identifies the recipient of the phone call; 打电话 is a verb-object expression.

请明天上午给老师打电话。 (qǐng míngtiān shàngwǔ gěi lǎoshī dǎ diànhuà.)

Please call the teacher tomorrow morning.

HSK entry 45
对不起 (duìbuqǐ)

sorry

fixed-expression · introduced

对不起，我来晚了。 (duìbuqǐ, wǒ lái wǎn le.)

Sorry, I came late.

对不起 is an apology; 来晚了 means arriving late, with 了 indicating the completed situation.

“对不起，我没有看见你。”她说。 (“duìbuqǐ, wǒ méiyǒu kànjiàn nǐ.” tā shuō.)

"I'm sorry, I didn't see you," she said.

Written dialogue: 对不起 is used naturally in a spoken exchange, rather than forced into formal prose.

Conventional apology; can be analyzed as a verb phrase, but functions as a fixed social expression.

HSK entry 120
没关系 (méi guānxi)

it doesn't matter

fixed-expression · introduced

没关系，我们再来一次。 (méi guānxi, wǒmen zài lái yí cì.)

It's okay; let's do it one more time.

“没关系，我们明天再来。”她说。 (“méi guānxi, wǒmen míngtiān zài lái.” tā shuō.)

"It's okay; we can come back tomorrow," she said.

Written dialogue: 没关系 is used naturally in a spoken exchange, rather than forced into formal prose.

Fixed reply meaning it is all right/it does not matter.

HSK entry 121
没事 (méishì)

no problem

fixed-expression · introduced

没事，我很好。 (méishì, wǒ hěn hǎo.)

No problem, I am fine.

没事 is conversational reassurance, meaning that nothing is wrong.

“没事，你可以回家。”他说。 (“méishì, nǐ kěyǐ huí jiā.” tā shuō.)

"It’s okay; you can go home," he said.

Fixed reassurance meaning nothing is wrong/no problem; other verbal uses exist.

HSK entry 147
你好 (nǐ hǎo)

hello

fixed-expression · introduced

你好！很高兴认识你。 (nǐ hǎo! hěn gāoxìng rènshi nǐ.)

Hello! Nice to meet you.

很高兴认识你 is a polite formula for meeting someone.

“你好，请问这里有人吗？”她问。 (“nǐ hǎo, qǐngwèn zhèlǐ yǒu rén ma?” tā wèn.)

"Hello, excuse me, is anyone here?" she asked.

Written dialogue: 你好 is used naturally in a spoken exchange, rather than forced into formal prose.

HSK entry 165
请 (qǐng)

please; invite

verb · introduced

请坐。 (qǐng zuò.)

Please sit down.

请坐 is a concise polite invitation to take a seat.

请在这里坐一下。 (qǐng zài zhèlǐ zuò yíxià.)

Please sit here for a moment.

Polite request/please, grammaticalized use of the verb invite/ask.

HSK entry 166
请问 (qǐngwèn)

may I ask

verb · introduced

请问，医院在哪儿？ (qǐngwèn, yīyuàn zài nǎr?)

Excuse me, where is the hospital?

请问 introduces a courteous question to someone you may not know.

“请问，电影院在哪儿？”她问。 (“qǐngwèn, diànyǐngyuàn zài nǎr?” tā wèn.)

"Excuse me, where is the cinema?" she asked.

Written dialogue: 请问 is used naturally in a spoken exchange, rather than forced into formal prose.

HSK entry 171
认识 (rènshi)

know (someone)

verb · introduced

我认识这位老师。 (wǒ rènshi zhè wèi lǎoshī.)

I know this teacher.

认识 means to know or be acquainted with a person, not just to know a fact.

学生们都认识这个老师。 (xuéshēngmen dōu rènshi zhège lǎoshī.)

All the students know this teacher.

HSK entry 218
喂 (wèi)

hello (on phone)

interjection · introduced

喂，你好！ (wéi, nǐ hǎo!)

Hello!

喂 is used to answer the telephone and is commonly pronounced wéi in that context, though wèi is also listed.

“喂，你好，我想找老师。”她说。 (“wéi, nǐ hǎo, wǒ xiǎng zhǎo lǎoshī.” tā shuō.)

"Hello, I’d like to speak to the teacher," she said.

Attention-getting interjection; phone usage may be wéi or wèi depending on context.

HSK entry 219
问 (wèn)

ask

verb · introduced

我想问一个问题。 (wǒ xiǎng wèn yí ge wèntí.)

I want to ask a question.

问 introduces a question; 一个问题 is “one question.”

课后可以问老师。 (kè hòu kěyǐ wèn lǎoshī.)

You can ask the teacher after class.

HSK entry 220
问题 (wèntí)

question; problem

noun · introduced

我有一个问题。 (wǒ yǒu yí ge wèntí.)

I have a question.

有问题可以问老师。 (yǒu wèntí kěyǐ wèn lǎoshī.)

If you have questions, you can ask the teacher.

HSK entry 241
谢谢 (xièxie)

thank you

verb · introduced

谢谢你帮我！ (xièxie nǐ bāng wǒ!)

Thank you for helping me!

“谢谢老师！”学生说。 (“xièxie lǎoshī!” xuéshēng shuō.)

"Thank you, teacher!" the student said.

Written dialogue: 谢谢 is used naturally in a spoken exchange, rather than forced into formal prose.

HSK entry 272
再见 (zàijiàn)

goodbye

fixed-expression · introduced

再见，明天见！ (zàijiàn, míngtiān jiàn!)

Goodbye, see you tomorrow!

再见 is the standard goodbye; 明天见 adds the expectation of meeting tomorrow.

“再见，下个星期见！”她说。 (“zàijiàn, xià ge xīngqī jiàn!” tā shuō.)

"Goodbye! See you next week!" she said.

Written dialogue: 再见 is used naturally in a spoken exchange, rather than forced into formal prose.

Conventional farewell used as a fixed social expression; also analyzable as a verb.

HSK entry 187
事 (shì)

matter; thing

noun · introduced

我有一件事要说。 (wǒ yǒu yí jiàn shì yào shuō.)

I have something to say.

件 can count matters or incidents; 事 means a matter or thing to discuss.

有事的时候，可以给老师打电话。 (yǒu shì de shíhou, kěyǐ gěi lǎoshī dǎ diànhuà.)

If something comes up, you can call the teacher.

Noun meaning a matter, thing, or affair (有事、一件事); a general concept rather than an action verb.

Usage and grammar

Responding to thanks

A fixed courtesy expression is the usual response to a thank-you.

不客气！ (bú kèqi!)

You're welcome!

不客气 is a standard spoken response to thanks; the short answer is a complete utterance.

Polite requests

A short politeness marker can make a request courteous.

请坐。 (qǐng zuò.)

Please sit down.

请坐 is a concise polite invitation to take a seat.
